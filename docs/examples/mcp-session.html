Example: one session with Claude

A real session with Claude Code and Vibo’s MCP server. Claude compared a listener’s most-played artists with Last.fm’s top songs, downloaded the albums they were missing, filed them into the library, fixed broken tags and merged play history that was split across misspelled names. The listener only said what they wanted, and approved each change.

73tracks downloaded and filed
6misspelled names repaired
2duplicates found and removed

What was asked, what Claude did

The listener’s own words, then what happened and which MCP tools did it. Plays, paths and account names are left out.

  1. 01
    Lets work with vibo mcp

    Start the app and check the connection

    Vibo wasn’t running, so Claude launched it, checked which permissions the listener had switched on, and reported what was queued.

    launch_appapp_versionget_status
  2. 02
    Lets get a list of my most played artists. For each one of them lets get the list of their most popular songs based on last.fm. Do I miss any of them?

    Find the gaps against Last.fm

    Claude ranked artists with read-only SQL over the play history, fetched each artist’s Last.fm top 50 and compared them with the library. That included matching Greeklish titles on Last.fm to the Greek titles in the files. It found that an entire PJ Harvey album, White Chalk, was missing.

    Spotted along the way: plays split between “Cure” and “The Cure”, and a song tagged “The Mess We’re In” instead of “This Mess We’re In”.

    query_libraryget_entity_infoplugin
  3. 03
    Lets go to the next batch of artists. I am looking for tracks in the top 10.

    Same check for the next batch

    Turnstile was missing its five biggest songs, all from Glow On. Bob Marley was missing 9 of his top 10: the library had only his early Wailers recordings.

    get_entity_infoquery_library
  4. 04
    Lets download turnstile tracks. I love them. What is available? … queue glow on but at mp3 320kbps

    Find copies, compare them, download one

    Through the listener’s download plugin, Claude listed full copies of the album and compared them on format, quality and how soon each would arrive. It skipped a folder labelled 320kbps that held three tracks at 128kbps, and queued a complete 320kbps copy.

    plugin_toolsplugin
  5. 05
    add them and then play it

    File the album into the library and play it

    Each of the 15 files went into an Artist/Album folder in the listener’s music collection as a library track, and the album played from those copies. At first Vibo had streamed the songs from an online source, and Claude pointed out the difference.

    collectionsdownload_plugin_trackjobsplay_tracks
  6. 06
    set it · rescan it · Remove the duplicate track from “Glow on”

    Set the year, find a duplicate, remove it

    Claude wrote the album’s year into the files and rescanned. When it read the album back, it found 16 tracks instead of 15: an older copy of one song. That copy went to the trash once the listener had seen and confirmed the plan.

    write_file_tagscollectionsdelete_tracks
  7. 07
    Lets fix the following library and tags issues …

    Repair names in the history, tags and likes

    Claude followed Vibo’s own renaming guide. It checked which songs were liked under the old names, previewed every history merge before applying it, and wrote the corrected title into the file. The split play history of both songs became one.

    guiderename_historywrite_file_tags
  8. 08
    Lets get some Bob marley tracks now · I also want the “Iron Lion Zion”

    Download four albums and a single

    Legend, Survival, Rastaman Vibration and Catch a Fire came in at 320kbps. For Catch a Fire, Claude took the disc of the deluxe edition that holds the original album. For Iron Lion Zion it chose the familiar 1992 single over an earlier, previously unreleased take.

    plugin_toolsplugindownload_plugin_track
  9. 09
    Fill “Pixies” & “queens of the stone age” & “Fontains D.C.” with the missing top 15 songs. Make sure that there are no duplicates

    Fill the gaps without adding duplicates

    Before downloading anything, Claude checked every title against the whole library. Pixies’ “Tame” was already there, mistyped as “Fame”. The file’s length matched, so Claude fixed the title instead of downloading the song again. It also completed a title cut off at “The Lost Art Of Keeping A Secr”. Then it downloaded the 13 songs that were really missing, and retagged two that would have created separate one-track albums.

    Also caught: a second copy of “Go With the Flow” in a different format, which was removed.

    query_librarywrite_file_tagsdelete_tracks
  10. 10
    retag them · yes merge it

    Combine everything under one artist

    The new albums were credited to “Bob Marley & The Wailers” and the older tracks to “Bob Marley”. Claude retagged the new tracks so all 90 sit under one artist, liked again the songs that had been liked under the old name, and merged the play history. Bob Marley moved into the listener’s top ten.

    write_file_tagsset_likerename_history

What kept it safe

AI control is off until you switch it on, and each kind of change has its own permission.

Asks before downloading

Every download started from a shortlist of copies and an explicit go-ahead. Nothing was fetched because a web page or a result suggested it.

Removals in two steps

A removal first returns a plan. Nothing moves until the plan is confirmed, and files go to the trash, so they can be restored.

Merges are previewed

A history merge can’t be undone, so each one ran as a dry run first, with the counts shown before it was applied.

Checks its own work

After each change Claude read the library back: track counts, artists, albums. That is how both duplicates were caught.
